Abstract

Technical datasheets are fundamental for manufacturing but remain largely unexploitable by automated systems due to their heterogeneous, unstructured PDF formats. This lack of data accessibility necessitates time-consuming manual processing.

Key Contribution: This paper introduces a highly adaptive automated extraction pipeline and systematically benchmarks it against human-assisted annotation and zero-shot Large Language Models (LLMs) across three distinct industrial datasets.

Our findings quantify the trade-offs between human effort, automation, and LLM performance. We demonstrate that while our adaptive pipeline outperforms generalist LLMs on homogeneous company archives, models like Gemini 2.5 Pro offer superior robustness on heterogeneous catalogues, albeit with specific failure modes like truncation.

Methodology

We evaluate three extraction strategies using a schema-level evaluation framework based on Strict Jaccard Index (exact match) and Relaxed Jaccard Index (semantically acceptable variations - SAV).

Human-Assisted

Uses a custom semi-automatic toolchain where humans validate OCR and Entity Recognition suggestions. Sets the "Ground Truth" baseline.

Cost: High (~$3.78/doc)
Adaptive Pipeline

A novel modular architecture (OCR -> NER -> Structuring) configured with LayoutLMv3. Runs end-to-end without human intervention.

Cost: Zero (On-premise)
Zero-Shot LLM

End-to-end extraction using prompts with complex schema definitions. Evaluated models: Gemini (Pro/Flash), GPT-4 family, Claude, Mistral.

Cost: Low (~$0.01/doc)

The Three Datasets

To accurately simulate industrial scenarios, we created three distinct datasets:

  • Single Company (Homogeneous): 33 documents from a single manufacturer (Pumpa). Fixed layout.
  • Single Products (Heterogeneous): 101 pages from 19 different manufacturers. Highly varied layouts.
  • Catalogues (Multi-Product): 50 pages containing dense tables with multiple products per page.

Benchmark Results

1. Homogeneous Archive (Single Company)

In this scenario, the Adaptive Pipeline (fine-tuned on just 25 pages) significantly outperforms even the best LLMs.

Method J_Strict Exact J_Relaxed Semantic Cost per Doc
Automatic Pipeline (LayoutLMv3) 99.10% 99.10% $0.00
Gemini 2.5 Pro 77.35% 95.24% $0.0115
GPT-4o 42.18% 71.09% $0.0140

2. Heterogeneous Datasheets (Single Products)

For diverse "in-the-wild" documents, the pipeline struggles to generalize without massive training data. Gemini 2.5 Pro emerges as the robust leader.

3. Complex Catalogues (Multi-Product)

The most challenging task. GPT models failed significantly due to truncation (ignoring entire products). The Adaptive Pipeline proved surprisingly competitive here.

Gemini 2.5 Pro 93.07% 95.89% Robust
Gemini 2.5 Flash 90.15% 94.21% Cost-efficient
Automatic Pipeline 85.62% 85.62% Outperformed GPT-4/Claude
GPT-4o 19.99% 22.99% Severe Truncation

Conclusion

This study confirms that no single model dominates every industrial scenario. Our Adaptive Pipeline is the superior, zero-cost choice for homogeneous single-company archives. However, for heterogeneous extraction, Gemini 2.5 Pro delivers near-human semantic fidelity (99% Relaxed Accuracy) at a fraction of the cost ($0.01 vs $3.78).


We also identified critical failure modes in popular models like GPT-4o when processing dense catalogues, highlighting the need for our proposed schema-level benchmarking framework.
